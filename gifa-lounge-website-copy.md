## 0. Brand voice notes (for you, not the site)

- **Tone:** warm, hospitable, unpretentious. Gifa's strongest reviews are about atmosphere, coffee and cocktails — lead with feeling, not with superlatives about the food.
- **Avoid:** "award-winning", "best in London", "fine dining". Nothing the reviews won't back up.
- **Use the culture:** injera, berbere, the coffee ceremony, eating from a shared plate. This is the differentiator on a road full of takeaways.
- **Reading level:** short sentences. A big share of traffic is mobile, at 7pm, deciding where to eat in the next 20 minutes.

## 9. Build recommendations

1. **Opening hours are the priority.** They're absent from every listing online. Put them in the footer of every page and update the Google Business Profile the same day.
2. **The drinks menu is the missed opportunity.** Cocktails are the most consistently praised thing in Gifa's reviews and appear nowhere on their current site. Give drinks a real section with photography.
3. **Lead with atmosphere in the photography.** Reviews are mixed on food and service but consistently warm on the room, the coffee and the cocktails. The imagery should sell the evening, not just the plate.
4. **Manage expectations on service.** If bookings are confirmed manually, say so. Don't imply instant confirmation the kitchen can't honour.
5. **Set the tone on first-timers.** Explicitly welcoming people who've never eaten with injera converts a whole category of nervous local customers.
6. **Mobile first, genuinely.** Hours, phone, address and directions should be reachable in one tap on a phone at 7pm.
7. **Existing site:** gifalounge.com is a WordPress build on a Slider Revolution theme, last touched around April 2025. Confirm before you start: are you replacing it or restyling it, who owns the domain and hosting, and where the DNS is managed.

### Dashboard UX

- **Not** a TanStack Table. ~30 items where sequence matters more than filtering — use a category accordion with drag-to-sort items inside each. Reserve TanStack Table for enquiries.
- Inline availability toggle on each row that PATCHes immediately. This is the control staff will actually touch daily; don't bury it behind an edit modal.
- Price input in pounds, coerce to pence in the Zod schema (`z.coerce.number().multipleOf(0.01)` → transform).
- Bulk action: "mark all available" — one click every morning.
- Guard `is_signature` at 3 in the schema, not just the UI.

### Zod sketch

```ts
export const menuItemSchema = z.object({
  categoryId: z.number().int().positive(),
  name: z.string().min(2).max(120),
  description: z.string().max(600),
  priceGbp: z.coerce.number().min(0).max(999),
  imageId: z.number().int().positive().nullable(),
  isVegan: z.boolean().default(false),
  isVegetarian: z.boolean().default(false),
  isSpicy: z.boolean().default(false),
  allergens: z.array(z.enum([
    'gluten','egg','milk','nuts','peanuts','sesame',
    'soya','fish','crustaceans','molluscs','celery',
    'mustard','lupin','sulphites'
  ])).default([]),
  isAvailable: z.boolean().default(true),
  isSignature: z.boolean().default(false),
});
```

The allergen enum is the UK's 14 declarable allergens — keep the list complete even if only a few are ever used, since it's a legal disclosure surface.

## 11. Booking feature

Public side is a single form. Dashboard side is a single table. No availability engine, no seat map, no calendar sync in v1 — Gifa confirms by phone and the site should be honest about that.

### `bookings`

| Column | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `id` | int PK | |
| `reference` | varchar(12) unique | short human code, e.g. `GF-8K2M` — give it to the customer on confirmation |
| `name` | varchar(120) | |
| `phone` | varchar(32) | required — this is how they'll actually be contacted |
| `email` | varchar(180) null | optional |
| `party_size` | tinyint | 1–30; above 12 nudge toward private hire |
| `booking_date` | date | |
| `booking_time` | time | |
| `occasion` | varchar(60) null | birthday, engagement, business, none |
| `notes` | text null | allergies, high chair, accessibility, first-timers |
| `status` | enum | `new` \| `confirmed` \| `seated` \| `no_show` \| `cancelled` |
| `staff_notes` | text null | internal only, never shown to the customer |
| `source` | varchar(30) | `website` — leaves room for phone/walk-in entries later |
| `created_at` | timestamp | |

### Public form

Fields: name, phone, email (optional), date, time, party size, occasion, notes. Superforms with progressive enhancement so it still submits without JS.

Validation worth enforcing server-side:

- `booking_date` not in the past, and not more than 90 days out
- time falls inside opening hours for that weekday, checked against the hours table (or a soft warning if hours aren't configured yet)
- honeypot field plus a rate limit by IP — a public form on a restaurant site attracts bots within days
- party size > 12 → response redirects to the private hire enquiry instead

Confirmation message must say **request**, not booking: "We'll confirm by phone shortly. For a table in the next couple of hours, please call 07950 786579." Promising an instant table the kitchen hasn't seen is how a website generates a bad review.

### Dashboard table

TanStack Table earns its place here.

- Default filter: `booking_date >= today`, sorted ascending. Staff want tonight, not history.
- Columns: date, time, name, party size, occasion, status, phone (click-to-call), notes indicator.
- Quick filters: Today / Tomorrow / This week / All.
- Status change inline via a select — no modal for the most frequent action.
- Row expand for notes and staff notes.
- Row highlight when `party_size >= 8` or `notes` is non-empty, so the kitchen spots the ones that need prep.
- Export to CSV for the week.

### Notification (non-optional)

Email on every submission to the restaurant address, and ideally WhatsApp to the booking number. If a booking only exists inside the dashboard, it will be missed. Build the notification in the same commit as the form.

### `gallery_tags` + `gallery_image_tags`

Free-form many-to-many. Tags are for search and public filtering (`injera`, `cocktails`, `birthday`, `coffee ceremony`, `live music`); `context` is structural and fixed. Keep the two separate — collapsing them into one field is the thing you'll regret at month three.

Tag input: shadcn-svelte Command combobox with create-on-enter, and a normalised slug so "Live Music" and "live music" don't become two tags.

### Dashboard UX

- Grid view, not a table. Multi-select with a checkbox overlay, bulk tag / bulk context / bulk delete.
- Drag-and-drop multi-upload straight into your existing browser-side compression before it hits the server.
- Alt text required on upload — block the save. It is far easier to enforce at upload than to backfill 200 images later.
- Filter bar: context, tag, place, featured, date range.
- "Used in" indicator on each image so nobody deletes the hero shot. Cheap query, saves a support call.
- Serve WebP with dimensions attached; lazy-load everything below the fold.

### Public gallery page

Filterable by tag, masonry grid, lightbox with caption and place. Images tagged `event` and linked to a past event get a "from [event name]" line in the lightbox — that's the quiet upsell into private hire.

## 14. Supporting controllers

**Opening hours** — `opening_hours` (weekday, open_time, close_time, is_closed) and `date_overrides` (date, open/close/closed, label). Highest-value data on the site; drives the footer, the contact page, booking-time validation and `openingHoursSpecification`.

**Site settings** — single-row key/value or a typed singleton: phone, email, address, social URLs, Just Eat link, hero heading and sub, delivery on/off.

**Announcements** — `announcements` (message, level, starts_at, ends_at, is_active). Sitewide banner for Eid hours, private-event closures, "kitchen closed, bar open". Trivial to build, and the thing clients use most.

**Enquiries / message viewer** — `enquiries` with `type` (`contact` \| `private_hire`), shared fields plus a JSON payload for type-specific ones (event date, headcount, event type), `status`, internal notes. Same notification rule as bookings.
